## Full description (4,000 characters max)

```
QIP Assist helps emergency medicine doctors in training plan, run and write up a quality improvement project (QIP), and helps their supervisors and departmental QIP leads follow and sign off that work.

It follows the Model for Improvement and is built around the Emergency Medicine Quality Improvement Assessment Tool (EM QIAT) used for the curriculum's quality improvement learning outcome.

For trainees:
• A setup wizard that checks your idea fits one rotation, with fifteen starter projects to adapt.
• An aim coach that reads your aim and says what is missing (number, date, place).
• Outcome, process and balancing measures with an operational definition template.
• Weekly data entry (or paste from a spreadsheet), run charts and SPC charts, with help reading them.
• Diagnosis tools such as driver diagrams and fishbone diagrams, each with a worked example.
• PDSA cycles with a required prediction, an action plan, a timeline, stakeholders and team.
• Surveys and feedback, a sustainability plan and a handover note for whoever takes the project on.
• A readiness check against the EM QIAT, and exports of your project as a report, an abstract or an A3 summary.
• A learning hub that explains each step in plain English.

For supervisors and QIP leads:
• Trainees add you to their project by your email address; you then see it in your overview.
• Review the project, leave feedback and record sign-off. Opening a trainee's project is recorded in an audit log.

Try it first: Demo mode on the sign-in screen opens a complete sample project with no account.

Optional AI suggestions use your own Google AI key, entered in Settings; without one, everything else still works.

Important:
• Do not enter patient-identifiable information. The app is for project data in aggregate (counts, percentages, times), not patient records.
• QIP Assist is an educational and portfolio tool. It is not a medical device and does not give clinical advice.
• It is independent. It is not produced, affiliated with or endorsed by the Royal College of Emergency Medicine (RCEM). Always check the current curriculum and your deanery's requirements.

You need an account (Google or email) to save your own projects. Projects are stored with Google Firebase, as the privacy notice explains, and you can delete your account at any time.

Published by West Midlands Evidence Based Emergency Medicine Ltd (EM Evidence).
```

Checked on 29 October 2026: the fifteen starter projects, the aim coach, driver and fishbone diagrams, the A3 export,
